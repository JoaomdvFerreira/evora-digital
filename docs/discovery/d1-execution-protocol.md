# D1 Execution Protocol — Institutional & Data Source Mapping

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft for D0 closure

## Objective

Build a systematic, cross-domain institutional evidence base and data-source catalogue without searching specifically for software ideas.

## Domain sequence

Start with the baseline taxonomy, but allow new domains to emerge.

Suggested first pass:

1. Mobility & Transport
2. Housing
3. Social Support
4. Accessibility
5. Urban Infrastructure
6. Public Services
7. Economy & Business
8. Employment & Talent
9. Education
10. Culture & Events
11. Environment & Waste
12. Climate & Energy
13. Health & Wellbeing
14. Tourism
15. Sports & Leisure
16. Geography & Territory
17. Digital Access & Services

The sequence may change based on evidence availability.

## For each domain, collect

### Problem Intelligence

- explicit problems;
- unmet needs;
- policy objectives;
- indicators;
- affected populations;
- relevant geographies;
- existing programmes/interventions;
- known stakeholders;
- previous public participation;
- unresolved or repeated concerns.

### Open Data

- source owner/publisher;
- candidate datasets;
- machine-readable formats;
- APIs/feeds/services;
- update frequency;
- licensing/reuse status;
- geographic granularity;
- identifiers and standards;
- possible data consumers.

## Search discipline

Avoid:

> “What apps does Évora need?”

Prefer source-oriented and domain-oriented questions:

> What mobility problems are identified in current municipal and intermunicipal plans?

> Which public transport datasets are published, by whom, in what format, and with what reuse conditions?

## Evidence recording

Every material claim should produce or update an `EVD-*` record.

Do not create duplicate `PRB-*` records for every source. Link independent evidence to the same problem where appropriate.

## Source priority

Prefer:

1. canonical public body/operator;
2. primary strategy/plan/report;
3. official statistics;
4. formal public participation;
5. credible research;
6. secondary reporting.

Secondary sources may identify leads but should not silently replace primary evidence when primary material is available.

## Coverage gate for a domain

A domain may move from active research to consolidation when:

- major current institutional plans/reports have been reviewed;
- key statistical sources have been checked;
- known major operators/interventions have been identified;
- obvious public data sources have been checked;
- stakeholder categories are known;
- repeated additional research is no longer producing new top-level problem clusters;
- unresolved questions are explicitly logged.

This gate means “sufficient for the next discovery stage”, not “complete knowledge of Évora”.
